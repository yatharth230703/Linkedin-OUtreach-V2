    Earlier last month, We at MoE Labs co-hosted a small AI eval discussion with New Enterprise Associates (NEA) because we kept running into the same question while looking at AI and agent companies:

    As models get better, how do you tell where progress is actually coming from - the model itself, or everything around it?

    We started to see eval less as a tooling problem, and more as a useful lens for separating model progress from system progress.

    A few observations from the discussion:
    1️⃣ Many production failures have little to do with raw reasoning ability.
    They tend to come from parsing, retrieval, tool calls, and how errors compound across a system.
    2️⃣ Teams rarely adopt eval to optimize performance.
    They adopt it after something breaks, when the cost of being wrong becomes hard to ignore.
    3️⃣ Single-step evaluations are relatively easy to reason about.
    Once systems become multi-step and agentic, understanding reliability - and failure modes - becomes much harder.
    4️⃣ In practice, the bottleneck is often not tooling, but data and judgment.
    Knowing what to evaluate and how to interpret failures matters more than dashboards.
    For us as early-stage investors, eval has been useful not just for judging product readiness, but for understanding where real progress in AI is happening - and where system design still dominates outcomes.

    We’ll be sharing a more structured breakdown of how we’re thinking about AI eval based on this discussion in a separate piece.
    Thanks to everyone who contributed to the conversation at MoE Labs × New Enterprise Associates (NEA).
