China posted a record trade surplus of $1.2 trillion in 2025 amid US tariffs. In the book Breakneck: China’s Quest to Engineer the Future, released last year, Dan Wang offers a useful lens to understand how the world’s two largest economies, China and the United States, function differently. China, he argues, operates as an “engineering state”, where leaders and institutions think like engineers, prioritising large-scale building, rapid industrial expansion, and centralized problem-solving. This contrasts sharply with the US’s more process-driven and rule-bound “lawyerly society”. This engineered flexibility helps explain how China weathered multiple rounds of US tariffs and still emerged stronger.

In today’s Plain Facts section of Mint, Prof. Jaydeep Mukherjee and I examine the forces behind China’s trade resilience and the lessons this holds for India.
