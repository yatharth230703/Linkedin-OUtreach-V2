IISc - CBR Research & Innovation (CBRAIN) Internship 2026, Bangalore, India::

Cohort Data Curation and Analysis

The Centre for Brain Research (CBR) invites applications for the CBRAIN Internship Program 2026. Now in its fourth edition, the program continues to provide an opportunity for students passionate about aging brain research.


This year, the program will focus on handling, integrating, and analysing large-scale multimodal health data drawn from publicly available datasets as well as CBR’s longitudinal cohort studies on brain aging. Under the mentorship of leading scientists and data analysis team members at CBR, the interns will develop practical skills in working with diverse data modalities and contribute to research aimed at advancing our understanding of the aging brain and age-related brain disorders. As part of the program, interns will also apply machine learning algorithms to predict cognitive impairment in large cohort studies using state-of-the-art datasets, including multi-omics data. Participants will gain hands-on experience in data science, multimodal data integration, and advanced analytical approaches.


We invite motivated and analytically minded students to join us in uncovering insights from complex health data.

Eligibility

The internship is intended for motivated applicants with a strong interest in research on brain aging and neurodegenerative diseases and a willingness to engage rigorously with multimodal datasets.
Applicants should be currently enrolled in the final year of a Master’s degree (M.Sc / M.Tech / M.E / MCA / equivalent) at an Indian institution, preferably in Biostatistics, Bioinformatics, Data Science, or related quantitative disciplines. Students from other academic backgrounds may also apply, provided they have demonstrable interest and experience in working with data, particularly in a health or biological sciences context. Experience in AI/ML will be viewed favourably.

Students in the final year of a Bachelor’s degree (B.Sc / B.Tech / B.E / equivalent) may also apply, provided the other criteria listed above are met.
Candidates with basic to intermediate coding proficiency in Python or R will be preferred.

Salient features
The internship duration is typically three months (preferably 1 May-31 July 2026). Shorter internships may not be feasible. In select cases, longer engagements will be considered on a case-by-case basis.
The internship will be conducted in person at CBR; remote participation or virtual enrollment will not be possible.
A consolidated stipend of INR 25,000 per month will be provided to Master’s students. Bachelor’s students will receive a stipend of INR 20,000 per month.
Interns are responsible for arranging their own accommodation and travel.
During the program, interns will be required to adhere to CBR’s working hours and other applicable institutional norms.

    Students’ Behaviour in 21st-Century Classrooms: Moving from Concern to Action

    In my previous post, I highlighted a growing concern many educators are experiencing — changing student behaviour in modern classrooms.

    While technology distractions, peer influence, and changing home dynamics play a role, the responsibility of shaping the learning environment still lies with us as educators.

    As a teacher and team leader, I strongly believe in learning from practice. The following strategies are not only theoretical ideas but approaches I have personally implemented and consistently observed being applied successfully by members of my team.

    ☆ Establish clear routines

    Predictable classroom structures help students feel secure and reduce unnecessary disruptions.
    I have observed this being applied very effectively by Mr. Saeed and Rajat Sawhney, whose structured classroom routines support smooth learning environments.

    ☆ Make learning active
    When students are involved in discussions, collaboration, and problem-solving, behaviour challenges naturally decrease.
    samra faisal and Andleeb Khalid consistently create engaging and active learning environments.

    Building teacher–student relationships: Students respond positively when they feel seen, heard, and respected.
    Walaa Farid and Amina Hameed
    has always demonstrated this beautifully through supportive and empathetic connection with students.

    ☆ Use technology with purpose
    Instead of competing with technology, channel it into learning through meaningful digital activities.
    NASLIN SITHARA consistently plans purposeful use of technology and AI to enrich classroom learning.

    ☆ Be consistent with expectations
    Clear boundaries and fair consequences build accountability and mutual respect.
    Shiny Mathew sets these expectations very clearly, creating a balanced and respectful learning environment.

    I feel fortunate to work with a team of educators who continuously demonstrate that effective classroom management is built through intentional strategies, collaboration, and commitment to students’ growth.

    Improving student behaviour is not about stricter control.
    It is about creating classrooms where engagement, respect, and responsibility become the norm.

    Small intentional practices by teachers can transform the classroom culture in powerful ways.
    I would also love to hear from fellow educators —
    What strategies have worked best in your classrooms?
