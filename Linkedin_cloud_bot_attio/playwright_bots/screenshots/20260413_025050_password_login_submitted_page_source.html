    Hiring a Quant from India?
    Your job description is probably screening out the best ones.

    Most German JDs look like a checklist for a machine:
    • 10+ years with specific legacy tools
    • A PhD from a Top 5 EU university
    • Willingness to work strictly 9-to-5 in HQ

    You’re not describing a pioneer. You’re auditing a librarian.

    The top Indian Quants aren't looking for another "stable" role.
    They've solved for stability. They’re solving for scale and intellectual velocity.
    They join a firm like 𝐗𝐘𝐙 𝐂𝐚𝐩𝐢𝐭𝐚𝐥 not for the job, but for the playground.

    Here’s the pivot.
    Stop listing requirements. Start presenting a problem.

    **Bad JD:** "Must know Python, C++, and have 5 years in derivatives pricing."
    **Compelling JD:** "How would you build a pricing model for an emerging market derivative where historical volatility data doesn't exist?"

    One asks for a history. The other invites you to make it.

    The data tells the story:
    At top Indian tech institutes, over 40% of engineering graduates specialize in fields directly applicable to quantitative finance—compared to ~27% in many Western markets.
    They're not just learning theory; they're competing in global algo-trading championships by their second year.

    Your checklist JD attracts applicants who are good at following maps.
    A problem-statement JD attracts the ones who draw new ones.

    The outcome isn't just filling a role.
    It’s gaining a strategic mind who can see around corners.
    This is how you move from hiring a "resource" to acquiring a decisive, long-term advantage.

    Let’s talk.
    What’s the most complex, unsolved problem on your trading desk right now?
    Put it in the JD and see who applies.
