Helping Lost Graduates Rebuild Their Careers | Founder @ Code At Random | Building Margdarshak.ai | Storyteller behind Lakshitography

1d • Edited •

🚨 “𝐖𝐞’𝐫𝐞 𝐚 𝐔𝐀𝐄-𝐛𝐚𝐬𝐞𝐝 𝐜𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐚𝐧𝐲 𝐡𝐢𝐫𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐢𝐧𝐭𝐞𝐫𝐧𝐬 𝐚𝐜𝐫𝐨𝐬𝐬 𝐈𝐧𝐝𝐢𝐚.”

Sounds impressive, right?

Now let’s decode what this often really means.

No Indian registration.
No GST.
Hundreds of “interns” working on sales, marketing, and operations.

Not employees. Not freelancers. Just… “interns.”

Let’s be honest.

If interns are:
• Driving your core business.
• Handling revenue-generating activities.
• Working with targets and reporting structures.

That’s not an internship model.

That’s a 𝐝𝐢𝐬𝐠𝐮𝐢𝐬𝐞𝐝 𝐰𝐨𝐫𝐤𝐟𝐨𝐫𝐜𝐞 𝐦𝐨𝐝𝐞𝐥.

And the “international company” tag?
Sometimes it’s less about global presence… and more about avoiding local accountability.

⚠️ Here’s the uncomfortable truth:
An internship is meant for learning — not for replacing a company’s workforce at scale.

When a company depends on interns to run operations, it raises real questions:
• Where is the mentorship?
• Where is the structure?
• Where is the compliance?

Because titles don’t define reality. Work does.

💡 To students:
Don’t get impressed by geography.
Get clarity on growth.

Ask:
“What skill will I walk away with in 30 days that I can’t learn on YouTube?”

If there’s no clear answer… you already have your answer.

💡 To founders:
Shortcuts in compliance and hiring might help you move fast…

…but they also make you fragile.

And fragile businesses don’t scale. They get exposed.

#Internships #StartupReality #CareerGrowth #Founders #Hiring #India

Executing the Ultimate AI Heist: Inside Invictus's Biggest Event✨✨
I am incredibly proud to share the success of HeistGPT - The Agentic Escape X CodeCrunch ML Edition, a flagship collaboration between NeuralAI DTU and IEEE DTU.

As Main Lead for the event, it was incredibly rewarding to drive the vision, technical direction, and on-ground execution of what went on to become the highest-footfall and highest-registered event across Invictus’26, the annual tech fest of DTU.

What made this event especially exciting was the depth of technical problem solving on display. A major highlight was the 𝐎𝐟𝐟𝐫𝐨𝐚𝐝 𝐀𝐮𝐭𝐨𝐧𝐨𝐦𝐲 𝐒𝐞𝐠𝐦𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐂𝐡𝐚𝐥𝐥𝐞𝐧𝐠𝐞, where participants worked on training robust semantic segmentation models for off-road autonomy. Instead of being limited by traditional data collection and annotation bottlenecks, teams worked with high quality synthetic data generated through Duality AI's Falcon digital twin simulation platform, building solutions designed to generalize to novel desert environments and optimize for real world deployment.

It was incredible to see participants go far beyond surface level model building, fine tuning architectures, handling class imbalance, benchmarking performance on unseen scenarios, and aggressively optimizing Intersection over Union (IoU) scores and inference speed. More than just a competition, the event became a space where applied machine learning, systems thinking, and engineering discipline came together in the best way possible. Special thanks to our judges for bringing sharp technical insight, thoughtful evaluation, and the kind of scrutiny that pushed participants to think deeper and build better.

Architecting an event of this scale required an exceptional team. A huge shoutout to my fellow leads, Unnat Agrawal, Hemant Singh, Jahan Sharma and Saksham Jain, for operating with absolute precision throughout. This would also not have been possible without the unwavering support and guidance of our faculty mentors, Dr. Dinesh K. Vishwakarma and Dr. Bindu Verma.
Grateful for the scale, proud of the technical depth, and even more excited for what we build next.

A massive thank you to our industry backers, Duality AI, Genesis, and Engineered Packaging Solutions, for powering this initiative and helping us bridge the gap between academic learning and cutting-edge industry practice. A special shoutout to our junior leads, Saksham Chhabra, Komal R., Navya Gupta & Lakshay Agrawal, for there flawless on ground execution.
